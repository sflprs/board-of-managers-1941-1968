Miss Woods and Miss Zane sold copies of "Inasmuch", the history of the Society from 1853 to 1953. Guests signed their names in a handsome leather bound book, the gift of Mrs. James Towne [?] whose firm had given us the paper for the invitations for the reception. In addition, many congratulatory telegrams and checks totaling $1706 were received from interested friends of the Society.

Two sisters, Miss Pasley [?] and Mrs. Seargent [?], reading in the newspapers the publicity given the Society in anticipation of the reception, visited the Home, and on Miss Ash's invi-tation came to the reception. Pleased with the spirit of the Home and the evident good housekeeping, they requested the Board to accept upon their death, their small houses in Mill Valley and their furniture, knowing that they would receive appreciation and care. This generous offer was accepted by the Board. Miss Pasley [?] and Mrs. Seargent [?] spent Christmas at the Home and enjoyed those memorable celebrations [struck through: and] good dinners for which Miss Ash and Ben, our cook, are justly famous.

In March, the "Family" accompanied by the Board and members of the Staff enjoyed a beautiful tea at Mrs. Krusi's home.

The Bazaar was held on November fourteenth and again was a great success. The "Family" and Convalescents are devoted to Mrs. Byrne and look forward eagerly to her companionship as well as the many pleasant afternoons spent working with her and Mrs. Allen, Mrs. Hilbert and Mrs. Krusi. Seventeen members of the "Family" received five dollars each from the Bazaar Fund at Easter and ten dollars at Christmas. Fifty dollars from this fund was used to suppliment the gift of one hundred dollars from the Ann M. Stovall [?] Alumnae Association to purchase a radio-phonograph. This year the Bazaar netted seven hundred and ninety-six dollars and forty-six cents after all deductions and Christmas gifts.

During the summer Mrs. Krick and Miss Ash discarded many obsolete volumes from the library and with fifty dollars from the Bazaar Fund, Mrs. Krick was able to purchase many new books. These are in great demand.

The "Family" has been in unusually good health during the year. On April first Mrs. Hale, a member of the "Family" for sixteen years, passed away at the age of eighty-seven. She is greatly missed by everyone.
