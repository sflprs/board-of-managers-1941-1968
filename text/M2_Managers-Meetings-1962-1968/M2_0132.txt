RECREATION: In the absence of Mrs. Rettenmayer, Mrs. Staples
reported that the hobby classes have been well attended; two of the
new residents, Mrs. Hewelcke and Mrs. Mauritius, are participating.
Mrs. Cox reported that the August Birthday Party was a great success-
She displayed colored photographs she had taken of each birthday
celebrant; each will receive one print, and, if more prints are de-
sired, the negative. Mrs. Cox is making arrangements for residents
to attend the Burke's School Festival, October 27, 1967; it may
also be possible for the Burke's Festival Play to be performed at
The Heritage. Mrs. Perry reported that plans for the Fall Tea are
going well, and requested that invitations be extended to boards
of other homes as soon as possible so that their members can plan
to attend. Invitations will be sent to people who have given
gifts to The Heritage. Miss Bridge requested an itemized report
of what is spent on the Tea.

LIBRARY: In the absence of Mrs. Kent, Mrs. Knight Smith re-
ported that she will donate Thornton Wilder's Eighth Day to the
Library.

"HERITAGE HAPPENINGS": Mrs. Charles Smith reported that she and
Mrs. Bailey will meet with Mrs. Bell and Mrs. Casper after lunch
to discuss "Heritage Happenings" and plan for the next issue.

VISITING COMMITTEE: Mrs. Perry and Mrs. Craig reported that
each had visited several residents. Mrs. Logan requested Board
members to visit residents whenever possible.

ADMINISTRATOR'S REPORT: Mrs. Bell reported that Mrs. Casper,
Social Director, is conduciing social hours for residents before
dinner and after musicales; sherry, port or juice is served. She
thanked Mrs. Nishkian for contributing cases of sherry and port.
Mrs. Casper is also conducting "Do you remember?" recording sessi-
ons for residents, in which Dr. Evans of the Historical Society

is most interested. Mrs. Bruck offered to donate the late Dr.
Bruck's Sony Tape Recorder if it is the type needed. Mrs. Van
Weynen has arranged several concerts, Mr. James Beasley presented
his one-man theatre, and Mr. Al Stone gave an accordion program.
Mrs. Metcalf suggested a trip for residents to the exhibit at the
Blace of Fine Arts which opens this week. Mr. and Mrs. Fillmore
were given a golden wedding anniversary party at lunch. Miss Ladd,
legally blind, has recently made a trip to Pittsburg. Mrs. Casper
is conducting classes in arm-chair exercises, with the approval of
Mrs. Hallum, Head Nurse. She has also bought a station-wagon and
takes residents to the Senior Center and elsewhere; the insurance
on the car is in order. Mrs. Bell called on Mrs. Krick who is very
ill. Mrs. Schilling and Mrs. Bell attended a Hospital and Nursing
flome Meeting. The proposed "panic button" from the administrator's
cottage to the lobby and the infirmary will not be installed because
it is too expensive.

OLD BUSINESS: Mrs. Nigh reported that the Building Committee has
received approval from the Board of Directors to investigate plans
and prices for tearing down the flats on Francisco Street and
building a recreation center. Tentative preliminary plans call
for a lestory building in the architectural style of the infirmary,
approximately 24' x 50' over-all, with seating for about 120
people, accoustical walls and ceiling, a stage, storage room, and

i a serving counter. The estimated cost is between $40,000.00 and
$60,000.00 for the building, not equipped. After discussion, it
was moved, seconded and carried that we -proceed with preliminary
plans for the recreation room. Also under consideration are two
single guest rooms with one or two baths on the third floor of the
Morgan building.

Mrs. Logan thanked Mrs. Craig, Chairman, Mrs. Bruck and Mrs.
Gunther for arranging the luncheon in honor of Miss Liston and for
selecting 4 pearl pin (purchased with money donated by Board members)
to be given to her. Remer Board members who have accepted the
luncheon invitation are Mesdames Hilbert, Landis and Lyon. Our
tnunar Administrator, Mrs. Pogson, has also accepted.
