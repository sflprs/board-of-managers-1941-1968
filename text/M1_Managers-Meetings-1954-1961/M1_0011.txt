12. Ques. Where would we stand if a major disaster befell the
Crocker home property during the new wing construct-
ion period?

Ans. We would have only a moral duty to try to help, for
the Crocker people would still be responsible for
the. care of their own even if they had to put them
in an hotel.

13. Ques. If the new wing is solely for the use of Crocker
interests, and the indebtedness means that in order
to pay back the loan and interest, we would not be able
to do the work we choose to do; what would the new wing
avail us?

Ans. The new wing is for the merged society and persons either
L.P.& R. or Crocker will be assigned rooms in either the
old or new buildings by the B. of M. Frankly, the whole
merger as far as the L.P. & BR. is concerned is a device
to get the facilities to carry on their wonderful work
on a larger scale. Infact, as I understand the policy
of the Society it is to take friendly care of older
people who need it and as the Crocker people need it
just as much as our "family" the Society is carrying on
its purpose; the only difference after the merger and
now is that some of the people for a while will not be
of your own selection but that in years to come it will
revert to its present family status, but on a larger
scale.

At the end of this period it was noted by Mrs. Craig that
during the past 10 years the Crocker Home has received about
$25,000. in bequests annually, and that we may hope to receive a
like sun.

Mrs. Bowes asked about the Van Ness property. Mr. Brown re-
plied that recently a $750,000. offer was turned down for the sale
of this property, and that next year when the present lease runs out
an advantageous conclusion will be reached by the Trustees.

Mrs. Hilbert asked if Government money could be realized for
use in building the annex. Mr. Hindes pointed out that then there
would be Government regulation and control, and that the whole pic-
ture would change.

Mr. Hindes stated that the rate of interest on a short term
loan would probably be at 33%, and that the money would be borrowed
only as needed and not in a lump sum, thereby minimizing the interest
due.

Mrs. Craig stated that there is generally a surplus at the end
of each year in the Mary Crocker Trust Fund which is distributed by
its Trustees, and in which we may hope to participate.

The per capita cost at the Crocker Home is $96.00 per month.

The question of an infirmary was raised, and it was pointed
out that the 10 room infirmary at the Crocker Home was found to be
adequate.

The gentlemen left and the regular meeting continued.

Mrs. Perry asked that all Christmas checks be sent in shortly.

Mrs. Carol Wilson will not be able to write the publicity for
the merger until after Christmas.

The Board looked at plans for the Annex.

There being no further business the meeting adiourmed.
